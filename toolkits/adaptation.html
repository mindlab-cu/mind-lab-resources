What the research shows

Brickman, P., Coates, D., & Janoff-Bulman, R. (1978). Lottery winners and accident victims: Is happiness relative? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 36(8), 917–927.
Comparing major lottery winners, ordinary controls, and accident victims, this classic study found present happiness converged across all three groups more than most people expect — winners weren't dramatically happier than controls, and victims weren't as devastated long-term as predicted.

What the research shows

Van Boven, L., & Gilovich, T. (2003). To do or to have? That is the question. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 85(6), 1193–1202.
Asked to compare a material and an experiential purchase from their own lives, most people pointed to the experience as the bigger source of lasting happiness.
Carter, T. J., & Gilovich, T. (2010). The relative relativity of material and experiential purchases. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 98(1), 146–159.
Satisfaction with material purchases tended to erode over time as people compared them to other options, while satisfaction with experiences held steady or grew — matching why "stays meaningful" items in this sort tend to be things you do, not things you own.
